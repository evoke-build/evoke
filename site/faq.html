What you install

01

A program, and the words to ask for it.

What exactly am I installing?

A reflex: a directory with a manifest, reflex.toml, and the program it names. The manifest says what the program does, what it is not for, and what to ask. The CLI and the SDK read that manifest to decide, and run the program.

evoke add owner/repo fetches a reflex from git at a version tag and pins it by content. No code runs at install time.

Can it use software I already have?

Yes, through a reflex you write. Its body can be a function, a script, a command with arguments, or a call to an API you use. evoke new writes a working one to start from.

An application you already have does not gain this on its own. Someone wraps one of its operations, and describes it.

Can it understand my own names and phrasing?

Yes. Your names go in a vocabulary: one word per room, service or person, with a meaning the classifier reads and a value the program receives. Your phrases go in an overlay, one line each. Both are files you own, and an update never touches them.

What can go wrong

02

A wrong reading is possible. A made-up value is not.

Can it make a wrong choice?

Yes. The classifier can misread a sentence and pick the wrong reflex, or the wrong option. What holds is the shape of every value: an option the author listed, a word from your vocabulary, a piece of what you typed checked against its range, or a yes/no switch.

What it chooses still passes the gate. A read runs at 0.6, a write at 0.8, and a destructive reflex always asks. Raise the bars for what matters, in a file you own.

What happens when a later step fails?

The steps after it are skipped, and each says so. Steps already done are not undone. The exit code is the worst step's. Before anything runs, the plan is shown, and a part that matches nothing refuses the whole sentence.

How are installed programs contained?

They are not, yet. There is no sandbox. A reflex runs with your permissions, in an environment scrubbed to five variables, and receives secrets only through its settings. Installing one is a trust decision. Read what you install.

What leaves your machine

03

One request, to the classifier you chose, with your key.

What runs locally, and what leaves the machine?

evoke runs on your machine. For each decision it sends one request to the classifier you chose, with your own key: your sentence, and the questions built from the installed manifests. Nothing goes to evoke.build.

A reflex is a program. It may contact its own service, and its description says what it does. The privacy policy names every party that sees something.

What does it cost?

evoke is free and open source, under Apache-2.0. Live decisions are billed by the classifier's provider under its own terms, with the key you bring. A sentence you repeat is answered from a cache and costs nothing.

What can I explore without setup?

The walkthrough on the home page and every example page. They replay recorded sessions, so nothing runs and no key is needed. Typing a sentence of your own needs the binary, a key, and reflexes installed.

How is this related to LLM tool calling?

The pattern overlaps. A model picks an operation and fills its arguments. evoke packages what sits around that: a reflex you install and share, a vocabulary of your own names, a decision your code can inspect before it runs, and a plan that runs several steps in order.
