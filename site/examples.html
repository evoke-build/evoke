Examples of natural-language commands you can run or adapt.

Type what you want, and a program you installed does it. A reflex is a small program you install and ask for in your words. One example runs today on a Mac, and five are patterns to adapt to your own programs.

Pick one, and follow it to its set-up.

Each page shows a sentence, what the terminal printed, one case where evoke asked or stopped, and what you need. Each ends with the steps to run it or adapt it.

$ evoke "set the volume to 40 percent"
01Everyday Mac tasksThirteen reflexes for everyday things on a Mac: lock the screen, set the volume, start a timer or a draft.Runs today on a Mac with the collection.
$ evoke "look up dana's address and email them"
02Pass a result to the next stepOne sentence looks up an address, and the next step starts an email to it.A pattern to adapt. You write the lookup. The mail reflex is in the collection.
$ evoke "scale checkout to 6 in staging"
03kubectl operations in your team's wordsYour names for services fill kubectl's arguments. A scale runs when evoke is sure enough, and a rollback always asks.A pattern to adapt. Needs kubectl and access to your cluster.
$ node queue.ts < inbox.txt
04Triage typed requestsA file of requests, one per line. The clear ones run, and the rest wait for a person.A pattern to adapt, at a terminal or in eleven lines of your app.
$ node runbook.ts "drain the primary"
05A runbook that asks before its destructive stepEach step's program names the next. Only the destructive step asks.A pattern for your app. The database programs are stand-ins.
$ node checker.ts
06Maker-checker approvalOne person types a payment. The decision waits until another confirms it.A pattern for your app. Authorization stays yours.

No example fits? Wrap a program of your own.

evoke runs only reflexes that someone wrote and you installed. A reflex can wrap a script, a command or a function you already have. To wrap a command, one file is enough: the manifest, which says what it does and what to ask you.
